How the Priority Score is calculated ▾

Priority Score = 0.35 × (recent 5-year avg, normalized) + 0.25 × (long-term avg, normalized) + 0.20 × (frequency across years, normalized) + 0.10 × (trend/change %, normalized) + 0.10 × (inverse volatility, normalized)

Each component is min–max normalized across the 9 subjects (0 = lowest of the 9, 1 = highest) before weighting, so the final score is a relative ranking among these subjects, not an absolute probability. "Volatility" here is coefficient of variation (std dev ÷ avg) — a lower CV (more consistent year-to-year marks) contributes positively.

This formula is a judgment call, not an official GATE methodology — the weights (35/25/20/10/10) were chosen to weight recent performance somewhat more than distant history, consistent with GATE's own periodic syllabus revisions. A different set of reasonable weights would shift the ranking somewhat, especially among adjacent-ranked subjects.

HISTORICAL YIELD · SUBJECT TIERS 🟢 Computed live from the dataset
This tab used to show 40 per-topic "ROI scores" (e.g. "BST Operations: 8.7") across four subjects. None of that was real — there's no per-topic marks or effort data anywhere in this project, so an ROI score at that granularity can't be computed honestly. What follows instead is a subject-level Historical Yield, which the dataset actually supports.
Historical Yield = 0.5 × (recent 5-year avg, normalized) + 0.3 × (frequency across years, normalized) + 0.2 × (inverse volatility, normalized). This measures marks contribution and consistency — it is not an effort-adjusted ROI, because study effort per subject isn't something this project can measure objectively (per-subject prep time varies hugely by student background). Historical frequency does not guarantee future appearance — GATE 2027's actual paper may differ from this pattern.
